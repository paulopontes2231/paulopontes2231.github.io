Rules

Conway's Game of Life is a cellular automaton devised by mathematician John Conway. It is a zero-player game, meaning its evolution is determined by its initial state, requiring no further input.

  • The game consists of a grid of cells, each of which can be alive or dead.
  • Each cell interacts with its eight neighbors (horizontally, vertically, and diagonally adjacent).

Rules:

  1. Any live cell with fewer than two live neighbors dies (underpopulation).
  2. Any live cell with two or three live neighbors lives on to the next generation (survival).
  3. Any live cell with more than three live neighbors dies (overpopulation).
  4. Any dead cell with exactly three live neighbors becomes a live cell (reproduction).

The game proceeds in steps called generations. At each generation, the rules above are applied to every cell simultaneously to create the next generation.

How to play:

  • Click on cells to toggle them alive or dead and set your initial pattern.
  • Press Start to begin the simulation.
  • Press Pause to stop the simulation at any time.
  • Use Randomize to fill the grid with a random pattern.
  • Use Clear to reset the grid.
